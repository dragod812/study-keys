The art of
focused reading

Turn a useful passage into something you can remember, explain, and put to work.

Attention comes first

Reading is a conversation with an idea. Slow down when a sentence changes the way you understand a problem. Give that sentence a place in your own words.

A highlight is most useful when it has a purpose. Capture the central idea, name a useful term, or mark a question you want to revisit.

Make the connection visible

Connect a new concept to something you already know. A short note can hold the comparison; a small drawing can show the relationship.

The goal is not to collect more passages. It is to build a clearer understanding.

Keep one good question

Notice what the author leaves unexplained. Write the question before searching for an answer, so your next step follows your curiosity.

Ideas become useful when we try them. Choose one small experiment and describe what would make you change your mind.

Return with purpose

Revisit your notes after some time has passed. Explain the idea without looking, then compare your explanation with the original passage.

Keep what helps you think. Revise what is unclear. Let your notebook grow alongside your understanding.
