Designing for concurrency

Good systems make their constraints visible. A queue gives each request a place to wait, while a concurrency limit protects the resources doing the work.

The most useful abstraction is a budget of simultaneous work. That budget should reflect the slowest shared dependency, rather than simply the number of available workers.

Start with a clear limit

Concurrency controls how many operations can make progress at the same time. Throughput measures how many operations finish in a given interval. The two are related, but they are not interchangeable.

Measure latency before changing the limit. When a dependency slows down, adding workers may increase contention and make the queue grow faster.

const capacity = 12;
const pending = new Queue();
await scheduler.run({ capacity, pending });

Queues and fairness

Fairness is a policy decision. A first-in-first-out queue is easy to explain, but separate workloads may need separate budgets.

Expand the operational tradeoffs

Admission control protects a shared dependency by rejecting work before expensive resources have been allocated. Queue depth is an early signal of sustained overload.

Retries need a budget too

Retries consume the same capacity as new work. A bounded retry policy with randomized backoff avoids concentrating recovery traffic into the same short interval.

Operational checklist

  • Measure the queue’s age as well as its length.
  • Separate interactive and background work.
  • Keep retries bounded.
  • Expose the active concurrency budget.
